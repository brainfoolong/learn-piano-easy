# 0002: Every song of ours comes in four rarities that a meter enforces

## Decision

- Every song we write is our own arrangement of a public domain tune, under the MIT License.
- Each song has four versions, named like the rarities of game items: Common, Rare, Epic and Legendary.
- The original score is the song's Mythic step, where the catalog holds it and rates it above Legendary.
- The rarities span the learner's first year of piano. Legendary is about a year of practice.
- A tool, the meter, measures every version against a fixed table of limits, and a test fails on any
  break.
- A version must earn its rarity: it fits its own limits and breaks at least one limit of the rarity
  below.
- Every version plays the song's hook note for note, and the meter checks it against the song's tune.
- Every version ships with a guide: a finger on every note, the start position, a practice plan, tips by
  bar and the tricky spots.
- The catalog's 1 to 10 difficulty stays. Each rarity takes a band of it, from 1 to 4.5.
- Our songs carry no lyrics.
- [docs/our-songs.md](../our-songs.md) holds the rules, until the meter's head comment owns the limits.

## Why

The learner wants to play songs they know, at the level they are at today. A song in four versions lets
them start on day one and come back to the same tune as they grow. Playing a known tune again at a
higher rarity shows them their progress.

The ratings in the catalog are set by hand, and nothing checks them. Four versions of one song multiply
that risk. A meter with written limits makes a rarity a measured fact, so a new song cannot drift.

The hook rule protects the song's identity. A Common version simplified too far stops sounding like the
song, and then it teaches nothing the learner wanted.

The user chose the rarity names, so the app feels like a game while it teaches. The Mythic step gives
the original pieces in the catalog a place at the end of each song's path.

## Consequences

- Each song is four MIDI files and one guide file, written by a tool from one source.
- The library shows a song once, with its four rarities, and the original where one exists.
- The app reads a guide: authored fingering, start keys, practice steps and tips by bar.
- The five beginner songs move into this form. Fingering and track settings a learner saved under their
  old files move to the new ones, so no work is lost.
- The limits are tuned once against Clementi and Burgmüller before the first song is written to them.

## Rejected

- **One version per song at a single difficulty.** The learner outgrows it, and finding a known tune at
  their level becomes luck.
- **Three versions.** The step from the first weeks to a full year is too big for one middle version.
- **Rating by hand, with no meter.** Four versions per song make the drift of hand ratings four times as
  likely.
- **Rarity from a single weighted score.** A score hides which limit a version breaks. Limits per measure
  name the problem, and the arranger can fix it.
- **Italian tempo words, from Adagio to Presto.** Educational, but a learner may read Presto as "the same
  song, faster".
- **A chili scale, from Mild to Fiery.** The user chose game rarities instead.
