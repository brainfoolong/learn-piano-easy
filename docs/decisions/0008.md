# 0008: The landing page plays the real player after one click, and shows its picture before

## Decision

- The site has one note renderer: the three.js player. The landing page's 2D falling notes are deleted.
- The player's core embeds into any box, in the top-down view at the box's full width.
- A landing box shows a screenshot of the real player until a click. The picture pans and zooms slowly
  by CSS, and holds still under reduced motion.
- One click loads the player and starts it with sound. Only one landing player runs at a time.
- A page whose purpose is to play, such as a lesson's song, loads the player without a click.

This replaces the landing-page decision that the hero's notes are drawn on a 2D canvas.

## Why

A visitor saw a different player on the landing page from the one they would use. Two renderers also
had to be kept in step.

The real player cannot load with the landing page. Measured on a production build, it adds 276 kB of
gzipped script to the page's 84 kB. three.js's bare renderer alone is 134 kB. The page already loads
184 kB of its 200 kB budget. A click keeps that budget, and a visitor who never asks pays nothing.

## Consequences

- The landing pianos no longer move or sound before a click.
- The landing page needs a picture per song and scheme, made by `tools/landing-pictures.ts`.
- The scene loads its effects only when a setting turns them on, so the embed stays small.

## Rejected

- **The player loaded with the page:** it breaks the byte budget by about 2.4 times.
- **The player loaded once the page is idle:** every visitor pays 276 kB and a WebGL context, even one
  who only reads.
- **The 2D notes as the preview before the click:** two renderers would remain, which is what this
  decision removes.
- **A video of the player as the preview:** it costs several hundred kilobytes above the fold, where a
  still picture costs far less.
