# 0009: A song is one card, and its versions are named by their difficulty

## Decision

- A song is one card in the song explorer. It counts once in the song count and in every tag total.
- A song of ours shows its versions as steps in its card's head. Its original score, where the catalog
  holds one, is the last step.
- A card shows the version the learner picked on it, else the one last played, else the easiest.
- A song passes a filter when one of its versions does. Its card then shows a version that passes.
- The four versions are named Beginner, Easy, Medium and Hard. The original score is named Original.
- The German names are Einsteiger, Leicht, Mittel and Schwer, and Original.
- A tagline says what its version asks, and carries no game joke.
- The learner reads a difficulty as a word, never as a number.
- The catalog's number from 1 to 10 stays inside the app. It sorts the cards, fills a card's meter and
  maps to a level.
- A level is one of six bands of that number. Beginner is below 2, Easy below 3, Medium below 4 and
  Hard below 5. Advanced is below 7, and Expert starts at 7.
- The first four levels are the bands the meter gives the four versions, so a version's name is its
  level.
- "Difficulty" is the explorer's one quick filter, and it offers the six levels. No filter by version
  exists beside it.
- The ids of the versions keep their names: `common`, `rare`, `epic` and `legendary`, and `mythic` for
  the original score.
- Everything that carries an id keeps its name too. That is a file such as `legendary.mid`, the
  `rarity` field, the `--c-rarity-*` tokens, `data-rarity` and the `legendary` badge's id.
- The `legendary` badge is named "Hard song".

This replaces the names of decision 0002. Its four versions, its meter, its limits and its guide stay.

## Why

The user found one song on four or five cards too much. A search for one tune filled the result with
its versions, and the song count took each version for a song.

The user wanted plain difficulty names instead of game rarities. A plain name says how hard a version
is, and needs no explanation.

The user wanted no level numbers shown. A number from 1 to 10 has to be explained before it helps, and
a word does not.

One set of words serves the versions and the filter. The meter keeps each version inside one band, so
the name on a step and the level in the filter always agree.

The ids keep their names because a learner's saved work is keyed by them: fingering, track settings,
favourites and plays. A rename would lose that work, or need a migration of every store that holds it.
The priority *Never lose or corrupt work* decides this.

## Consequences

- The song count and every tag total count songs, so each is lower than the catalog's count of rows.
- The original score of a song of ours shows on that song's card alone. It leaves its composer's own
  pieces in the explorer.
- A card's star is the song's. It is on when any version is a favourite, and taking it off clears every
  version.
- A level keeps our versions of that level and every other song in its band. The tag "Our arrangements"
  beside a level keeps our versions alone.
- The catalog's titles carry the English names, since the song tool writes them once for every locale.
- The code keeps a vocabulary of ids the learner never reads. `RARITY_LEVELS` in `src/library/guide.ts`
  maps each id to the level that names it.
- **Performance:** the explorer groups the rows into songs once, when it reads the catalog. Each redraw
  passes over the songs once more, to choose the version each card shows. It draws fewer cards for the
  same catalog.
- **Memory:** the explorer holds one entry per song, and the learner's picks until the page is left.
- **Usability:** the learner sees each song once, and reaches each version in one click on its card. A
  version the card does not show hides its length and tags until that click.
- **Simplicity:** the learner's model is one song, one card and one scale of words. The implementation
  pays for it with two vocabularies: the ids, and the names a learner reads.

## Rejected

- **A select menu on the card instead of steps:** it hides the versions until it is opened, and a pick
  takes two clicks. Steps show every version of a song at a glance.
- **The names Easy, Medium, Hard and Expert:** Expert oversells an arrangement a first-year learner
  plays.
- **Numbered levels:** the user wanted words alone, and a number has to be explained before it helps.
- **A filter by the catalog's number:** it would show the learner a number they read nowhere else.
- **Renaming the ids and the files:** a learner's saved work is keyed by them, so a rename risks that
  work.
